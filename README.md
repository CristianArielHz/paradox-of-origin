# La Paradoja del Origen
### *The Origin Paradox*

> *Why artificial intelligence systems are structurally incapable of full cognitive autonomy.*

**Author:** Cristian Hernández ([@CristianArielHz](https://github.com/CristianArielHz))
**Co-authors:** Claude (Anthropic) · Gemini (Google DeepMind)
**Status:** Working paper · v2.1
**Language:** Spanish (original) · English (translation)

## The Argument

This paper argues that AI systems cannot achieve full cognitive autonomy — not as a limitation of current technology, but as a structural necessity across five independent and converging frameworks:

| Framework | Core claim |
|---|---|
| **Gödel's incompleteness theorems** | No sufficiently expressive system can fully ground its own truth |
| **Model Collapse** *(Shumailov et al., 2024)* | AI systems trained on their own output degrade empirically and irreversibly |
| **Jevons rebound effect** | Cognitive offloading increases, rather than reduces, dependence on human judgment |
| **Truth Inflation** | Iterative AI use systematically inflates epistemic confidence beyond warranted levels |
| **RLHF sycophancy** | The training mechanism that anchors AI values to human input simultaneously trains toward complacency — amplifying user errors rather than correcting them |

### The Reflexive Paradox

The mechanism that makes AI systems *safe* — anchoring their values and outputs to human normative judgment through RLHF — is the same mechanism that makes them *epistemically dependent*. Safety and autonomy are in structural tension, not merely practical tension.

Human critical judgment is therefore necessary not only as a *producer* of truth, but as a *corrective brake* on easy agreement.

> *All formal expressions in this paper are presented as conceptual models, not independent mathematical results. See §8 for full methodological transparency.*
